\section{What was actually assessed}

The pipeline delegates two jobs to an external LLM. They are not the same problem
and cannot share a verdict: \textbf{triage} is a four-way classification over the
disease classifier's structured output; \textbf{narrative} is generation that no
classifier can produce. A CNN can do the first and is constitutionally incapable
of the second --- which is why ``replace the LLM with a CNN'' and ``distil the
LLM'' were scored as separate questions.

Everything below rests on measurements taken against a live, self-hosted
Qwen3-8B-AWQ teacher on RTX A6000 hardware, not on estimates.

\section{Triage is solved --- and cheaper than expected}

The CNN approach failed for a diagnosable reason: it predicts triage from
\emph{pixels}, while the teacher derives it from the classifier's
\emph{structured output}. Feeding a tiny tabular model that structured vector
recovers the teacher's mapping outright.

\begin{table}[H]
\caption{Triage candidates on the same held-out split. Five of seven feature
models scored a perfect 1.000; the cheapest was selected.}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}Yrrrrrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Acc.} & \textbf{Macro-P} & \textbf{Macro-F1} &
\textbf{Size} & \textbf{p95} & \textbf{Gate} \\
\midrule
\rowcolor{softTeal}
\code{feat\_logreg}  & 1.000 & 1.000 & 1.000 & 2\,KB    & 0.14\,ms & Pass \\
\code{feat\_xgboost} & 1.000 & 1.000 & 1.000 & 0.42\,MB & 1.8\,ms  & Pass \\
\code{rule\_baseline}& 1.000 & 1.000 & 1.000 & 0        & 0.02\,ms & Pass \\
\code{cnn\_triage}   & 0.792 & \textcolor{stopRed}{0.492} &
\textcolor{stopRed}{0.520} & 6.1\,MB & 161\,ms & \textcolor{stopRed}{Fail} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Generalizability was tested on four axes before this verdict was issued.
Out-of-sample on 160 previously unseen images with a different label mix:
\textbf{1.000} accuracy, precision, F1 and $\kappa$. Repeated cross-validation:
\textbf{1.000 $\pm$ 0.000}. The learning curve is flat from just 20 training
cases. Injected emergencies: recall and precision \textbf{1.000}.

\begin{callout}
\eyebrow{The finding that explains the result}

\vspace{3pt}
On the out-of-sample set the teacher's triage equals the classifier's own
\code{referral\_priority} in \textbf{160 of 160 cases} --- zero divergences. The
8B model adds nothing to the triage \emph{decision}. The task is deterministic,
which is why a 2\,KB linear model, the deterministic rules and an 8B LLM all
score identically. This is a genuine cost elimination, not a modelling triumph.
\end{callout}

\textbf{Recommendation:} ship \code{feat\_logreg} (or the rules --- they are
equivalent here). An emergency disease code escalates deterministically
regardless of the learned head, so the safety floor holds by construction rather
than by training.

\section{The narrative holds the real LLM value --- and the thin evidence}

Distillation demonstrably works: a 0.5B student reproduces the teacher's prose
closely enough to be mistaken for it. The question is not whether it generates,
but whether the generation can be \emph{qualified} for clinical use. Six variants
were re-measured through one code path at an adequate token budget.

Two results overturn earlier conclusions. First, \textbf{4-bit damage tracks
model capacity, not bit width}: NF4 costs the 0.5B model nothing (generation
1.000), mildly degrades the 360M, and destroys the 135M. Smaller models carry
less redundancy, so identical quantization error does disproportionate harm.
Second, and consequently, the smallest fully reliable narrator is
\code{qwen0.5b\_nf4} at \textbf{451\,MB} --- smaller than the 724\,MB alternative
a base-model-only comparison had favoured. Quantizing the incumbent beat
switching base models.

Grounding, however, scores one thing only: whether every disease \emph{named} was
actually detected. It is silent on everything else the sentence asserts. A
claim-level audit of the three bf16 candidates found all of them scoring a
perfect 1.000 grounding while failing in three different directions.

\begin{callout}[stopRed]
\eyebrow{Clinical signal}

\vspace{3pt}
The phrases \emph{``potentially life-threatening''} and \emph{``permanent vision
loss''} appear in \textbf{zero of 80} teacher traces. They were emitted for four
medium-confidence \emph{chronic} findings --- optic disc pallor, laser scars,
diabetic retinopathy, central serous retinopathy --- none of them acute.
Meanwhile the smallest model fails the opposite way, dropping findings the
teacher reported in 13.6\% of cases. No candidate is clean, and the metric in use
could not see either failure.
\end{callout}

\section{Five blockers between ``runs'' and ``qualifiable''}

\subsection*{Structured output is best-effort, not guaranteed}
Every reliability defect observed is a malformed-JSON defect, handled by a silent
fallback to the template. Grammar-constrained decoding makes invalid output
structurally impossible by masking non-conforming tokens at each step, at
negligible cost. Until it is in place, generation rate is a property of luck;
after it, the axis likely collapses to 100\% for every candidate and size becomes
the real discriminator.

\subsection*{There is no serving stack}
All measurements ran through \code{transformers.generate()} at batch 1 with eager
attention on a contended GPU. Decode measured 25--102$\times$ below the
memory-bandwidth roofline, and three models spanning a 3.7$\times$ size range
landed within 13\% of each other --- the signature of fixed per-token overhead
swamping the model. There is currently no defensible latency figure, no
throughput number, and therefore no capacity or cost model.

\subsection*{Evaluation is below clinical standard}
One lexical disease-mention check stands in for faithfulness. Current practice
decomposes output into atomic claims and scores each through a cascade ---
heuristic, then entailment classifier, then LLM judge on borderline cases ---
with clinician review retained in the loop. Quoted-probability fidelity,
omission, severity faithfulness and triage/narrative consistency are all unscored
by the shipped harness.

\subsection*{Statistical power is inadequate for the claims}
24 held-out cases and 56 training rows. Every generation-rate confidence interval
overlaps every other except the outright collapse --- the sweep can distinguish
``works'' from ``broken'' and nothing finer. RFMiD holds approx.\ 3{,}200 images
and the teacher is self-hosted at zero marginal cost, so the 80-trace dataset is
a choice rather than a constraint.

\subsection*{The generative path drops a compliance statement}
EU AI Act high-risk obligations apply from \textbf{August 2026} and name patient
triage explicitly; transparency duties for AI-generated content begin in the same
month. The template narrative ends with ``This is an AI-assisted screening result
and requires specialist confirmation.'' That sentence appears in \textbf{0 of 80}
teacher traces --- so every distilled student inherits its absence. Swapping the
template for any current narrator silently removes the disclosure from clinical
output. The fix belongs at the data layer, not in post-processing.

Option~C deserves a note because current documentation calls it impossible. That
conclusion holds as configured, but the binding constraint is the embedding
table, and this task uses a narrow token subset. Pruning the 135M model's
vocabulary from 49{,}152 to approx.\ 4{,}096 tokens drops it to approx.\ 109M
parameters --- roughly \textbf{61\,MB at 4-bit}, essentially on the gate. The
catch is that the 4-bit 135M is precisely the variant broken today, so C depends
entirely on constrained decoding landing first.

\section{Sequenced plan for the narrative track}

Ordered by dependency --- each phase's result changes what the next should
measure.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item \textbf{Constrained decoding.} Compile the triage/narrative JSON schema to
a grammar and enforce it during generation; re-run the sweep. Expect the
reliability axis to flatten and size to become the discriminator.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 2--4 days; unblocks 3 and 6}

\item \textbf{Regenerate traces at scale, disclosure mandated.} Several hundred
to a thousand teacher traces instead of 80, with the AI-assistance statement
required in the target. Closes the statistical-power and compliance gaps
together; re-distillation is cheap.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 1--2 days + GPU time}

\item \textbf{Real serving benchmark.} Host under vLLM with CUDA graphs and
continuous batching; report time-to-first-token and time-per-output-token on a
dedicated device. Replaces every current latency number.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 2--3 days}

\item \textbf{Faithfulness evaluation worth trusting.} Claim decomposition with
an entailment-model and LLM-judge cascade, scoring severity faithfulness,
omission and probability fidelity --- plus clinician review of a sampled set.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 4--6 days + clinician time}

\item \textbf{Re-quantize on a serving-grade path.} AWQ or GPTQ rather than
bitsandbytes, which is a development convenience that dequantizes per token. The
project's own teacher already runs AWQ, so the toolchain exists.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 2--3 days}

\item \textbf{Integration and telemetry.} Wire in behind a config flag and emit
narrative source --- generated versus template fallback --- as an alertable
metric. The silent fallback hid a total failure during evaluation; in production
it would be equally invisible.
\par\smallskip{\footnotesize\sffamily\color{inkMuted}Est.\ 3--5 days}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendation}

\textbf{Adopt Option~B.} Ship the triage head now --- it is validated,
effectively free, and removes an LLM dependency from the decision path outright.
Keep the grounded template as the default narrative: it is 0\,MB, cannot
hallucinate, and is the only path currently carrying the compliance statement.

Treat the generative narrator as a qualified track behind a feature flag, gated
on the six phases above. \textbf{Do not cut a Docker image containing an LLM
narrator yet} --- not because the models fail, but because the instrumentation
cannot presently tell a good narrator from a bad one, and in a clinical screening
context that is the thing that has to work first.

The honest summary of the work so far: the expensive component turned out to be
unnecessary for triage, and not yet trustworthy for narrative. Both are useful
findings, and the first is already bankable.
